# About the Founder

Hi. I'm Yuna.

I built Lighthouse because I got tired of the thing it solves, and because nobody else seemed to be building it, and because I had a long weekend that I had originally meant to spend on holiday and instead spent on a laptop in my apartment. The early prototype was so bad I considered throwing it away. The second prototype was better. The third one is the one you can use today.

I am based in Seoul. I work from home, mostly, with one day a week at a small co-working space near my apartment so I remember what other humans look like. I take meetings in English or Korean. My English is fine. My Korean is much better.

## The short biography

- Six years building developer tools, the last three of them at a public software company you've heard of. (I'll happily tell you which one in conversation; I'd rather not write it here because it implies endorsement and there isn't one.)
- Before that, two years at a startup that didn't make it. We were too early. I learned more from that startup than from any of the years before or after.
- Computer Science degree from a Korean university. Nothing on my CV before that worth bragging about.

## What I'm good at

Shipping. Picking the right next problem to work on. Talking to customers and getting them to tell me what they actually want, not what they think I want to hear.

I am a meaningfully better engineer than I was three years ago, which is the direction you want. I am a meaningfully worse engineer than the engineer I am about to hire, which is also the direction you want.

## What I'm not good at

Sales. I have never sold anything cold. The eight paying customers all signed up themselves, and I converted them by being helpful in their inbox, not by closing them on a call.

Hiring. I have hired exactly zero people. I have an idea of how I want to do it. I have a list of references I will check on every candidate. I will probably make some mistakes here and the cap table tells you I haven't tried yet.

Patience. I find it hard to slow down. The first version of this document had three paragraphs about my impatience. I cut them because they were embarrassing.

## How I think about Lighthouse

I think Lighthouse is the kind of product that doesn't need to be huge to be good. If Lighthouse ends up serving five thousand people very well, and those five thousand people are devoted to it, and the unit economics work out, I will consider Lighthouse a success.

I would rather build that than chase a billion-dollar outcome and miss. I am explicit about this with everyone I talk to, including would-be investors. Some of them tell me this is the wrong way to think about it. I have heard them out. I still think it's the right way to think about it for *this* product.
