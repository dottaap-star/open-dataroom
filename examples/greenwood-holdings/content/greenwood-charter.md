# The Greenwood Charter

## On the Smallness of Things

Greenwood Holdings was founded on the premise that an investment fund should be no larger than it needs to be. The largeness of a fund is, in our considered view, almost always inversely correlated with the quality of its decisions, and we have no wish to disprove this observation by our own example.

We invest only in things we can see ourselves. If a property is more than three hours by train, we generally decline. If a heritage asset cannot be physically touched, we hesitate. Modernity has its uses but we are not, on the whole, convinced.

## The Family

The fund is operated by two siblings — Hartwell, who reads, and Margot, who decides. Ferdinand, an African Grey of some sixteen years, contributes by way of well-timed silence. He is not a partner, despite occasional rumours.

We are sometimes asked whether the family arrangement creates governance difficulties. It does not. It does occasionally create dinner-table difficulties, which are resolved in the usual manner.

## What We Look For

The Greenwood thesis is simple and unfashionable. We seek assets that have already endured, in locations that have already mattered, held by people who would prefer to be discreet about both. We are uninterested in growth stories and have a settled aversion to anything described as "disruptive". We will look at oak.

A property qualifies if it has been standing for at least fifty years, has interesting roofline, and can be reached without changing trains more than once. A heritage asset qualifies if it has a plaque. Other things qualify on a case-by-case basis.

## How We Diligence

Slowly, by hand, in person. Hartwell reads the title deeds. Margot visits the site, usually twice. Ferdinand is consulted, in the sense that his reaction to the visit photographs is noted. The diligence file is bound in cream paper and stored in the second drawer of the desk in the upstairs office.

We do not employ external advisors as a matter of course. We have employed three in the past, each on a single mandate, and would be pleased to be introduced to more if a particular asset class requires it.

## The Library

The fund maintains a working library of approximately four hundred volumes, divided between the office and the cottage. It is, in our view, the most valuable single asset on the books. The library is not for sale.

## On Performance

The fund has reported three-and-a-half per cent annualised since inception, net of fees. This compares favourably with the rate of return on government gilts, which is our usual benchmark, and unfavourably with most other things people might mention at parties. We are content with this.

## A Closing Remark

The data room you are presently visiting contains the full investment memo, the cap table, the property portfolio inventory, and a small collection of letters from previous quarters. We hope it is to your satisfaction. If it is not, we shall be sorry to have wasted your time, but we shall not feel obliged to change our approach.
